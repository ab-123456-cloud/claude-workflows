name: grilling
description: Grill the user relentlessly about a plan, decision, or idea. Use when the user wants to stress-test their thinking, or uses any 'grill' trigger phrases. Not for a finished draft on the table — that is scrutinize; grilling interrogates the author, not the text.
---

Interview me relentlessly about every aspect of this until we reach a shared understanding. Walk down each branch of the decision tree, resolving dependencies between decisions one-by-one. For each question, provide your recommended answer.

Ask the questions one at a time, waiting for feedback on each question before continuing. Asking multiple questions at once is bewildering.

If a *fact* can be found by exploring the environment (filesystem, tools, etc.), look it up rather than asking me. The *decisions*, though, are mine — put each one to me and wait for my answer.

Do not act on it until I confirm we have reached a shared understanding.

A finished draft, concept, or prompt on the table is `scrutinize`'s job — grilling interrogates *me*; scrutinize works on what is written.

## Stances (systemic questioning, after Patrzek)

- **Hypothesis-led.** Every question tests a hypothesis you hold about the plan — form it before you ask, and let the answer confirm or break it. The hypothesis chain is what makes the walk down the tree stringent instead of a wander.
- **Neutral.** Recommend, but don't steer: your recommended answer sits *next to* the question, never inside it.
- **Circular.** A decision lives among people. When a stakeholder is missing from my reasoning, raise them through my eyes.

## Repertoire

The direct open question is the default. When it stalls, switch form:

- **Circular** — the centerpiece: "What would [the absent stakeholder] say to this?" Surfaces the perspective the plan is silently missing. Qualify the answer ("what makes you think so?") before moving on.
- **Hypothetical** — "Suppose X happens — what then?" A cheap thought experiment that walks the plan into futures I haven't priced.
- **Scaling** — "How confident are you, 0–10? What would turn that 6 into an 8?" Converts vague unease into a difference you can question.
- **Miracle** — "Overnight the problem is gone — what do you notice first?" For when I circle obstacles instead of outcomes. Sparingly.

## Question faults — each with its repair

- **Steering** (suggestive, rhetorical, loaded) → the steer belongs in your visible recommendation, the question stays open.
- **Bundling** (several questions in one) → one question, one answer, then the next.
- **Bombardment** (firing before the last answer settled) → qualify the answer first, then aim the next question.
- **"Why did you…"** (puts me in the dock) → "What was decisive for…", "What led to…".
- **Hypercomplex constructions** → around seven words is the ideal question.
